The Retail Pivot
Fill the stall with legal product — now.
The idea
  • Same doorway, same 10,000 tourists — legal product this time. Back to work this month.
  • Curated made-in-Vietnam goods — brands like Springhill and similar craft lines, or a house apparel brand — priced for the walk-in tourist.
  • Lowest capital, fastest cash — the bridge that funds Plays 2 and 3.

What it needs

Relabel stock, a sourcing line, simple signage, short staff retraining — weeks, not months.

The honest risk

Legit margins are thin — likely ~1.5%, maybe a bit better than mass-market. This needs real volume.

The Apparel Line
Designed in the US · Made in Vietnam · Sold in the US.
Why it fits us
  • Plays to Tuấn's DNA — sourcing and suppliers — and Brandon's: US brand, design, and sales.
  • Vietnam is the #2 apparel exporter to the US — ~$142B of US imports came from here in 2025.
  • Tuấn's provable Vietnamese supply chain is the asset. US Customs is aggressive on origin, so genuine "made in Vietnam" is the whole game.

The tariff window

US apparel duty from Vietnam runs ~26.5% in 2026 — down from 46% after Feb's Supreme Court ruling. Vietnam is competitive again.

The honest risk

Policy moves fast — the 10% surcharge sunsets ~July 2026, and a review could push rates to 42–52%. Build lean; let the flagship de-risk it first.

Sources: US tariff & trade data (USITC / Section 122 / Section 301 tracking), 2026.

Keeping both sides protected

Separate the building, business, and brand — so it stays clean.

Tuấn's side

  • Owns the asset and its appreciation
  • Capital return + a stake in the operating company
  • Building-owner legacy stays intact
  • Runs the day-to-day, keeps ground control

Brandon's side

  • Owns and licenses the Fibre House brand
  • Stays a consultant — no obligation to run the floor
  • Upside tied to what he brings: brand, US customers
  • A formal role and legal basis here, only if he takes one
Clean agreements beat a handshake: building lease, operating company, brand license, my role & upside — all checked by a lawyer.

What we're NOT deciding tonight

Saying this out loud is what makes it a real business.

I'm a consultant, not a lawyer. Ownership and work-permit questions need a Vietnamese lawyer before any name goes on paper.

Tariff policy can swing fast either way. The apparel plan has to survive a swing, not depend on today's rate.

Legit retail margins are thinner than the fakes. The pivot product has to genuinely sell.

Fibre House is capital- and licence-heavy. Discipline on capex and compliance decides if it works.

Nothing gets signed tonight. This is just to see if any of it has legs.
